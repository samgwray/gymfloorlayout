# Space-planning research: clearances, aisles, zoning, support space, ceilings, flooring

Topic: `space`. Research date: 2026-10-28. Covers task items (a) to (g). Every number is either quoted from a source in the source list at the end, or marked **[EST]** with its derivation.

**Source tiers used below**
- **T1, primary.** Standards and codes (ADA 2010 / DOJ, ISO 20957-1 text via the official preview and a test report, NSCA standards and checklists), DoD UFC 4-740-02, and manufacturer manuals and spec sheets (Precor, Life Fitness, Matrix, Star Trac/Core, Technogym, Assault, Keiser, NuStep, Concept2, StairMaster).
- **T2, secondary but expert.** Insurer and legal analyses (CPH & Associates, Sadler, Redwoods), HFA (formerly IHRSA), IDEA, and a peer-reviewed audit (Sekendiz et al. 2016).
- **T3, low.** Exam-prep summaries of NSCA and ACSM textbooks, vendor blogs, and SaaS blogs. Use these only as corroboration or as weak priors.

**Constraints on the research**
- The ACSM 5th-edition book (2019), ASTM F2115/F2216/F2276 and ISO 20957-6 / EN 957-6 are paywalled. The ASTM store also prohibits AI use of its standards.
- Their values are therefore taken from manufacturers, insurers and test reports that quote them. Each such case is flagged.
- The session's shared web-search budget ran out partway through. Some planned look-ups are listed under Gaps (section 11).

## 1. (a) Floor space per piece of equipment

### 1.1 ACSM (Health/Fitness Facility Standards and Guidelines)
The book itself could not be accessed. The current edition is the 5th; ACSM's page confirms the edition but gives no numbers. Edition-specific values come from secondary sources:

| Edition | Value | Source and evidence | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3rd ed. | 4th ed. (2012) | **40–60 sq ft per piece of equipment**; **10–14 sq ft per member** (whole facility per member); **40–60 sq ft per group-exercise participant**; *"a 3-foot buffer around each piece of equipment is expected"* | IDEA, Schrenk, "Facility Design and Construction 101, Part One" (2013, updated 2023), citing ACSM 2012 4th ed. | medium |
| Unspecified (course notes) | "Minimum required equipment clearance" for continuous-motion equipment: **sides 1.6 ft, back 3.3 ft**. "Industry practice ~40–60 sq ft per piece". "2017 IHRSA report: facilities ~9–15 sq ft per person" | Brainscape facility-design flashcards (student notes) | low |

The 1.6 ft / 3.3 ft pair equals 0.5 m / 1.0 m. Those are the ASTM F2115-05 values (see §2.2). ACSM-derived course material therefore appears to echo the 2005 ASTM figures, which were superseded in 2012.

Other claims of "ACSM" values found on blogs (e.g., Rush Walter: "minimum 50 sq ft per cardio piece and 50–80 sq ft per strength station") could not be traced to an edition. They are treated as low confidence.

### 1.2 NSCA

**Calculation table, primary (NSCA Strength and Conditioning Professional Standards and Guidelines, Strength & Conditioning Journal 39(6), Dec 2017, Appendix 4, Table D1 "Calculations for space needs").** Calculation summaries:

- **Prone and supine exercises** (bench press; lying triceps extension): add 3 ft to the 6–8 ft bench length and to a 7 ft user-space width. The 6 ft example gives (6 + 3) × (7 + 3) = 90 ft².
  → **bench_press_station / incline / decline: 90 sq ft** (6-ft bench) to **110 sq ft** (8-ft bench).
- **Standing exercises** (biceps curl, upright row): add 6 ft to the 4–7 ft bar length, then multiply by a 4 ft user-space width. With a 4 ft bar, (4 + 6) × 4 = 40 ft².
  → **40–52 sq ft** per standing barbell or dumbbell exercise station.
- **Standing exercises in a rack** (back squat, shoulder press): add 6 ft to the 5–7 ft bar length and use an 8–10 ft station width. At the upper ends, (7 + 6) × 10 = 130 ft².
  → **power_rack / half_rack: 130 sq ft** (range 88–130).
- **Olympic lifting area** (power clean): increase both dimensions of a typical 8 × 8 ft platform by 4 ft for the walkway allowance: (8 + 4) × (8 + 4) = 144 ft².
  → **lifting_platform: 144 sq ft**.

**Conflict.** NSCA's 2013 TSAC exam-prep module (also primary NSCA) works the bench example as *"(6 + 3 + 3) x (7 + 3 + 3) = 156 ft²"*. That is, it applies the 3-ft cushion on both sides of each dimension.
- **Recommended handling:** apply a 3-ft cushion on every open side and let the cushions of adjacent stations overlap. This gives 156 sq ft for an isolated bench and approaches 90 sq ft per bench in a row. Use 90 sq ft as the planning value.

**NSCA safety checklist, primary (from ESTC 4th ed., 2016; adapted from NSCA's Essentials of Personal Training).**
- Machine area and aerobic area: *"Easy access to each station (a minimum of 2 feet [61 cm] between machines; 3 feet [91 cm] is optimal)"*.
- Free-weight area, same wording plus: *"Olympic bars properly spaced (3 feet [91 cm]) between ends"*.
- Ceiling: *"Ceiling height sufficient for overhead exercises (12 feet [3.7 m] minimum) and free of low-hanging apparatus"*.
- Mirrors: *"Mirrors placed a minimum of 20 inches [51 cm] off the floor in all areas"*.

**NSCA TSAC module 5.0 (2013), primary.**
- "Ceiling height 12-14 ft of clearance".
- "Mirrors 20 in. from floor, 6 in. from equipment".
- "Passageways 36 in.", "Hallways 60 in.".
- Flooring: carpet *"Recommended for cardio areas"*; rubber *"Recommended for free weight areas"* (rolled $, locking $$, poured $$$); wood *"Recommended for Olympic platforms"*.
- "Tallest equipment and dumbbell racks – Placed along walls".

**ESTC aerobic-machine areas, secondary.** These come from three independent exam-prep summaries (PT Pioneer CSCS ch. 23, Achievable CSCS, a blog chapter summary) of *Essentials of Strength Training and Conditioning*, 4th ed.:
- stationary bikes **24 sq ft (2.2 m²)**
- stair machines **24 sq ft**
- rowers **40 sq ft (3.7 m²)**
- treadmills **45 sq ft (4.2 m²)**
- "skiers" 6 sq ft (0.6 m²). This is a legacy cross-country ski machine and the value is probably a transcription artifact. Do not use it.
- stretching/warm-up **49 sq ft** (7 × 7 ft)
- walkways 36 in; circuit-training walkways **4–7 ft**; platforms and racks 3–4 ft apart; 36 in of walking space around racks; weight trees 36 in apart near plate-loaded machines.

NSCA-CPT summary: stretching 40–60 sq ft per user; perimeter walkways 3–4 ft; free weight vs machine areas separated by 3 ft; resistance equipment 2–3 ft apart.

## 2. (b) Safety clearances from standards and manuals

### 2.1 ISO 20957-1 (general, all stationary training equipment)

- **Definitions, summarized** (ISO 20957-1:2013, official iTeh preview):
  - 3.2 *training area*: the equipment footprint together with the space the exercising user moves through.
  - 3.4 *free area*: additional space for getting on or off, including an emergency exit from the equipment.
- **Clause 5.17 d), summarized:** the manual must describe at least **0.6 m** of extra clearance on access sides, account for emergency dismounts, and show both areas in a diagram. Adjacent units may share that clearance. Sources: CCTS test report to EN ISO 20957-1:2013 (Report CCTS210727002SX, 2021, p. 8 of 12) and the Align-Pilates CE test report.
- **Model rule:** free area = training area + **0.6 m (23.6 in)** on every access side, shareable between adjacent units.
- ISO 20957-1:2024 (3rd ed.) has superseded 2013. Whether the 0.6 m figure changed was not verified (see Gaps).

The standard text was not accessible. Manufacturers implementing it state:

**Liability evidence (T2).** In *Jimenez v. 24 Hour Fitness* (2015), as summarized by HFA (2019):
- The owner's manual required *"three feet wide by six feet deep"* behind the belt.
- None of the 21 treadmills at the club had a 6-ft zone.
- The plaintiff hit a machine about 3 ft 10 in behind the treadmill. The appellate court treated the gap as a jury question on gross negligence.

**Field compliance (T2, peer-reviewed).** Sekendiz et al. 2016 (*Journal of Fitness Research* 5(1):24–28) audited 11 Australian facilities:
- Side and rear clearances were below manufacturer minimums ("0.5 - 1m on the sides, 2m behind") in **all 11 (100 %)**.
- **60 %** had equipment within 2 m behind treadmills.
- Conclusion: the packer must enforce these clearances as hard constraints.

**Recommended treadmill rule:**
- rear **79 in × unit width**
- sides **≥ 19.7 in**, with **24 in** planned between adjacent treadmills (shared) and to walls or other equipment at row ends
- front **0 in** for motorized units with the console facing a wall or window. Curved non-motorized units get **20 in** in front (Assault).
- Strict mode: sides 39.4 in (Star Trac).

The rear zone must be free of equipment and walls. Allowing a circulation aisle to coincide with the rear zone is common practice but is a judgment call (people walk into it). Flag it in the UI and offer a strict mode that keeps the zone exclusive.

## 3. (c) Accessibility, aisles and circulation

### 3.1 ADA 2010 Standards (DOJ), primary, verbatim
- **206.2.13:** *"Exercise machines and equipment required to comply with 236 shall be on an accessible route."*
- **236.1:** *"At least one of each type of exercise machine and equipment shall comply with 1004."*
  - Advisory: *"Most strength training equipment and machines are considered different types … there are many types of cardiovascular exercise machines, such as stationary bicycles, rowing machines, stair climbers, and treadmills. Each machine … is considered a different type"*.
- **1004.1:** *"Exercise machines and equipment shall have a clear floor space complying with 305 positioned for transfer or for use by an individual seated in a wheelchair. Clear floor or ground spaces required at exercise machines and equipment shall be permitted to overlap."*
  - Advisory: *"One clear floor or ground space is permitted to be shared between two pieces of exercise equipment."*
- **305.3:** *"The clear floor or ground space shall be 30 inches (760 mm) minimum by 48 inches (1220 mm) minimum."*
- **403.5.1:** clear width of walking surfaces *"36 inches (915 mm) minimum"*. Exception: 32 in for ≤ 24 in of length, with segments separated by ≥ 48 in.
- **403.5.2:** at 180° turns around elements < 48 in wide: 42 in approach / 48 in at the turn / 42 in leaving (not required if 60 in wide).
- **403.5.3:** routes < 60 in wide need passing spaces of 60 × 60 in at intervals of **200 ft max**.
- **304.3.1:** turning space of 60 in diameter (or a T-shape within a 60-in square).
- **Exception 8 to 205.1:** *"Exercise machines and exercise equipment shall not be required to comply with 309"* (operable parts).
- **Locker rooms:**
  - 222.1: 5 % of dressing/locker rooms (≥ 1 per cluster) comply with 803.
  - 225.2.1: ≥ 5 % of lockers, and no fewer than one of each type, are accessible.
  - 903.3: benches 42 in long minimum, 20–24 in deep.
  - 604.3.1: 60-in clearance at the water closet.
  - 608.2.1: transfer showers 36 × 36 in.

**DOJ Guidance on §§236/1004 (2010):**
- Machines used standing still need clear floor space.
- *"It is permissible to locate the clear floor space for such machines or equipment in the aisle behind the device"*, and it may overlap the accessible route.
- Accessible equipment can be arranged so that one accessible route serves more than one piece.

**Model constraint:**
- For every equipment type present, at least one unit must touch an aisle of ≥ 36 in that connects to the entrance.
- That unit needs a 30 × 48 in clear floor space beside the seat (transfer) or centred on the operating mechanism. For standing equipment the clear floor space may lie in the aisle behind it.
- Two machines may share one clear floor space.

| Rush Walter (blog, 2025) | main aisles 36 in minimum, 48 in preferred; emergency pathways ≥ 5 ft; benches 4 ft clearance; squat racks and platforms 6 ft |

**Recommended [EST]:**
- **main aisle (spine) 60 in.** This meets the ADA passing and turning requirement without separate passing spaces and matches NSCA's 60-in hallway.
- **secondary aisles 48 in** (36 in hard minimum, ADA/NSCA)
- **circuit-row aisles 48–84 in** (NSCA 4–7 ft)
- small floors (< 1,500 sq ft) may use a 48-in main aisle if 60 × 60 in passing spaces are provided.

### 3.3 Circulation share
- UFC sample worksheet: *"Net-to-gross Factor @ 0.2"*, i.e., gross = 1.2 × net, covering walls, corridors and mechanical space at building level.
- No primary source states circulation as a percentage of the training floor itself. A blog citing "ACSM 2007" mentions +10–15 % circulation; this was seen only in a search summary and was not verified.

### 3.4 Geometric check [EST]
- **6,000 sq ft floor (60 × 100 ft):** one 6-ft spine along 100 ft = 600 sq ft (10 %), plus two 4-ft cross aisles × 54 ft = 432 sq ft (7.2 %). Total **≈ 17 %**.
- **1,500 sq ft floor (30 × 50 ft):** one 4-ft aisle × 50 ft = 200 sq ft (13 %), plus an entry and turn area ≈ 60–100 sq ft. Total **≈ 17–20 %**.
- **Recommended:** reserve **15 % (large floors) to 20 % (small floors)** for aisles, on top of per-unit clearance envelopes. Rear treadmill zones and ADA clear floor spaces may overlap aisles only where permitted (see §2.4 and §3.1).

**Derived recommendations [EST]**
- **Treadmill zone ≥ 9 ft.** Design user 76 in (6'4", assumption) + 11-in step-up + ~6-in incline rise at mid-deck (20 % × 30 in) + ~4-in running vertical oscillation + 6-in head margin = **103 in ≈ 8.6 ft → 9 ft**. This matches LF's 9-ft PowerMill requirement.
- **General cardio floor ≥ 10 ft** wherever full-size stepmills are planned (StairMaster). With only treadmills, bikes and ellipticals, 9 ft is enough.
- **Free-weight, overhead and platform zones ≥ 12 ft, 14 ft preferred** (NSCA / UFC).
- **Rigs ≥ 12 ft.** Rig 105 in + ~36 in for an athlete's torso above the bar in muscle-ups/kipping ≈ 141 in = 11.75 ft.
- **Wall balls ≥ 12 ft.** 10-ft target + ball diameter and over-throw; consistent with NSCA's 12-ft overhead rule.
- **Plyometrics / box jumps:** 12–14 ft (NSCA TSAC/ESTC rationale: room for jumping and Olympic lifts).
- **Model behaviour:** if the user's ceiling is below a zone's threshold, exclude or penalize that equipment. Examples: no stepmills below 10 ft (Jacobs Ladder 7.5 ft and StairMaster 4G 8 ft remain possible); no rigs, wall balls or overhead platforms below 12 ft.

## 9. Conflicts and resolution

1. **Treadmill rear zone: 1 m vs 2 m.** ASTM F2115-05 required 1 m; F2115-12 and later, EN 957-6 practice, Precor, LF, Matrix, Star Trac, Technogym and Assault all use 2 m. Older course notes ("3.3 ft") echo the 2005 value. **Use 2 m.**
2. **Treadmill sides: 0.5 m (ASTM/EN, Precor, Assault) vs 0.6 m (Matrix T3xe) vs 1.0 m (Star Trac 10TRx) vs 3 ft (True blog).** **Use 24 in shared, with a strict option of 39.4 in.**
3. **Bench station area: 90 sq ft (NSCA Standards 2017) vs 156 sq ft (NSCA TSAC 2013).** The difference is whether the cushion is counted on one side or both. **Use 90 in rows and 156 when isolated.**
4. **Per-piece allowance: ACSM 3rd 25–50 vs ACSM 4th 40–60 vs UFC 50/65 vs ESTC 24–45 (cardio) vs Glofox 50 / 60–70.** The packer should use geometric envelopes; these allowances are for sanity checks only.
5. **Elliptical rear: Precor 0.5 m (or front) vs LF 2 m × 1 m.** **Default 24 in; strict 79 in.**
6. **Mirror bottom height: NSCA 20 in vs UFC 18 in.** **Use 20 in.**
7. **Ceiling for free weights: NSCA checklist 12-ft minimum; TSAC 12–14 ft; UFC 12 minimum / 14 preferred.** These agree. **Use a 12-ft hard minimum for overhead zones and 14 ft as the "ideal" flag.**
8. **Zone splits: sources disagree and are weak.** Treat as priors (§4.3).
